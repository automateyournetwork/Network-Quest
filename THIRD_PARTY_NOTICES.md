## Software

Three.js renders the rooms. The game’s generated schema validators call Ajv, ajv-formats and fast-deep-equal. Vite and Rolldown emit JavaScript helpers into the browser build. The downloadable register includes their complete upstream notices. It also identifies the three additional packages in the production npm lock used by contributor tooling, the twenty packages in the production PHP service lock, and the MIT-licensed Composer autoloader by Nils Adermann and Jordi Boggiano. The generated PHP autoloader and dependency files retain their upstream notices in a service distribution. [Software register](public/credits/notices.txt) · [Complete dependency inventory](licenses/DEPENDENCIES.md)

Three.js incorporates shading work by Eric Heitz, Jonathan Dupuy, Stephen Hill and David Neubelt. Their *Real-Time Polygonal-Light Shading with Linearly Transformed Cosines*, ACM Transactions on Graphics 35(4), 2016, supplies the area-light table source. The full copyright, redistribution conditions and citation accompany the compiled renderer. The register also preserves source notices for Earcut, glMatrix, Geometric Tools, Filament and the validation-expression sources credited in ajv-formats. Its URI expressions reference Jeff Roberson’s published derivation of RFC 3986. [Paper and authors](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/415-2/) · [Lighting license](licenses/runtime/ltc.txt) · [Source revisions and notice digests](licenses/runtime/sources.json) · [URI validation reference](https://jmrware.com/articles/2009/uri_regexp/URI_regex.html)

The interface uses system fonts, including Georgia where available. The production bundle has no font-face rules or font files. Some source-side review sheet scripts use local Arial, Helvetica or Pillow’s default face to rasterize labels. Their rendered labels and scripts accompany their respective batches. Font programs are absent from the indexed media source files. Production tools, voice embeddings and model weights have separate source records. ## Images, models and sound

John Capobianco directed the project. OpenAI Codex assisted with original construction scripts, animation, composition and edits. Blender produced the models, room compositions and sprite sheets. OpenAI image generation produced the room layers, portraits and teaching illustrations using the recorded project references. The original and registered pixels, known generation metadata, prompts, input permissions, changes and source lineage are recorded per asset. The project makes no exclusive claim over generated output. [Media register](public/credits/media.html) · [Manifest](assets-src/manifest.json) · [Dated output-terms review](docs/evidence/spec001/licensing.md#openai-production-image-terms)

ACE-Step 1.5 generated instrumental music from original prompts. Its code and selected weights carry MIT terms. The preserved publisher model card covers commercial generated music. The team selected, edited and mixed the output. Authored code generated room ambience and effects with synthesis and seeded noise. Audacity 4 and FFmpeg handled editing and exports. [Music source and notices](assets-src/audio/T124/NOTICE.md) · [Ambience](assets-src/audio/T126/NOTICE.md) · [Effects](assets-src/audio/T128/NOTICE.md) · [Pinned tool and model retrievals](assets-src/audio/auditions/rights/retrievals.json)

Kokoro 0.9.4 and hexgrad’s Kokoro-82M model produced the selected synthetic speech from original scripts. Their respective code and model terms are Apache-2.0. The narrator uses af_heart, Titan am_michael, Photon af_bella and Hermit am_fenrir. The model publisher credits training contributions including Koniwa tnc (CC BY 3.0) and the SIWIS corpus (CC BY 4.0), with additional acknowledgements in the model card. Model provenance records the voice hashes and the preprocessing dependencies’ own terms, including Misaki, spaCy, phonemizer and eSpeak-NG. The project attributes its scripts, direction and edits separately from model and voice rights.
